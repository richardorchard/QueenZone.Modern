# Queen: A Visual Documentary — Trivia Candidates

- Brian May's home-built "Red Special" guitar (nicknamed the "Fireplace" guitar in this book) cost just £8 in materials: the body is solid mahogany carved from the surround of a 200-year-old fireplace, and the tremolo unit's springs were salvaged from an old motorbike. He built it with his father Harold because he couldn't afford a Fender Stratocaster. (p.5)
- The book's cover/title page bills it as the "Wembley Memorial Concert Edition" of "Queen: A Visual Documentary" by Ken Dean (edited by Chris Charlesworth), published by Omnibus Press, "Featuring the Life & Times of Freddie Mercury 1946-1991" - first published 1986, this edition 1991/1992 to coincide with the Freddie Mercury Tribute Concert.
- At Ealing College of Art, Freddie Mercury's contemporaries included Pete Townshend of The Who, Ron Wood of The Faces/Rolling Stones, and Roger Ruskin Spear of the Bonzo Dog Doo Dah Band.
- Before settling on "Queen", the band (per band legend recounted in the book) went through six bass players in rapid succession before finding John Deacon as the "lucky seventh".
- Planned 1982 Queen concerts at Manchester's Old Trafford and London's Arsenal football grounds (May/June) were cancelled because all portable chemical toilets in the country had been booked up for cities being visited by Pope John Paul II. The London date was replaced by a concert at Milton Keynes Bowl (June 5) and the Manchester date moved to Leeds United's Elland Road (May 29).
- At the Freddie Mercury Tribute Concert (Wembley, 20 April 1992), David Bowie ended his segment by reciting the Lord's Prayer on one knee to underline the AIDS-awareness purpose of the show, then a partial Spiders From Mars/Mott the Hoople/Queen reunion played 'All The Young Dudes', with Bowie on saxophone, Ian Hunter on vocals, Mick Ronson on guitar, and Brian May, Roger Taylor and John Deacon backing them.
- During a De Lane Lea/Sarm session, Sid Vicious of the Sex Pistols reportedly stumbled into a neighbouring studio and needled Freddie about "bringing ballet to the masses"; Freddie shot back that they were "trying our best, dear." (p.38-39)
- To mark EMI International Records receiving the Queen's Award to Industry, the label pressed up 300 copies of 'Bohemian Rhapsody' on Royal Purple vinyl. (p.39)
- Film producer Dino de Laurentiis approached Queen to score his 1980 remake of the 1930s Flash Gordon serial - the band's first film soundtrack. (p.41)
- Brian May's 1983 solo mini-album 'Star Fleet Project' featured Eddie Van Halen and REO Speedwagon's Alan Gratzer, born out of an all-day studio jam session. (p.72)
- The 'Radio Ga Ga' video incorporates footage from Fritz Lang's 1927 silent film Metropolis, after Giorgio Moroder (who owned the film rights) asked Queen for a song in exchange for use of the footage. (p.73)
- Queen were originally approached about playing Live Aid by their touring keyboardist Spike Edney, who doubled as trombonist in Bob Geldof's band The Boomtown Rats. (p.83)
- The 'One Vision' video shows the band filmed working in their Munich studio in the same head-and-shoulders setup they'd appeared in on the 'Bohemian Rhapsody' video exactly ten years earlier. (p.86)
