The day Queen stole Live Aid

Twenty-one minutes on a July afternoon in 1985 that rewrote the rules of the stadium show.

13 July 1985·8 min read·Archive feature

Wembley Stadium was already three hours into the longest day in the history of popular music when Queen walked out to a piano, a drum riser and seventy-two thousand people who had come to see somebody else.

What followed has been described, endlessly and not inaccurately, as the finest live performance the band ever gave. The set was cut to the bone — six songs, no indulgence, every chorus a hook the stadium already knew.

"They understood the assignment. Nobody else that day did."

Rehearsals had been unusually disciplined. The band spent three days at the Shaw Theatre in London reducing twelve years of material to a twenty-one minute argument for their own greatness, timed to the second.
